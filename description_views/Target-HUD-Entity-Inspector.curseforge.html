Target HUD Entity Inspector

Look at any mob or player within 16 blocks to see its name, a 10-segment health bar, its health numbers, its type and how far away it is.

Shows the entity under your crosshair on the Action Bar with a bar such as ||||||.... beside the numbers. Neutral mobs you hurt switch to Aggressive, which suits players who fight near Wolves, Bees or Endermen.

About

Aim at a mob or another player and a single line appears on the Action Bar. It disappears as soon as you look away or a block is in the way. Nothing changes in vanilla combat, items or mobs.

What You Get

Health Bar
A bar of 10 segments shows how much health the target has left, next to the exact numbers, for example 14/20.
How It Works: A Zombie at 14 of 20 health shows seven red segments and three grey ones.

Mob Type
The line names the type as Player, Hostile, Passive, Neutral or Aggressive, each in its own color.
How It Works: Hit a Wolf, an Iron Golem or an Enderman and its type changes from Neutral to Aggressive.
Good To Know: Aggressive lasts one minute after your last hit on that mob, then returns to Neutral. Mobs hurt by something other than a player stay Neutral.

Distance Readout
The line ends with the distance to the target in blocks, for example 5m.

Armor Points
When you look at another player, their armor points appear beside their health.
Good To Know: Points are counted from the Leather, Copper, Golden, Chainmail, Iron, Diamond and Netherite armor they wear, plus a Turtle Shell helmet.

Tamed Pets
Your own tamed Wolves and Cats show as Tamed, in their own color, instead of Neutral or Passive.
Good To Know: Pets tamed by other players keep their normal type.

Instant Clear
The line is removed right away when you stop looking at the target or a wall blocks your view.

How to Play

  1. Enable the Behavior Pack in your world's settings.
  2. Look at a mob or player within 16 blocks.
  3. Read the name, health bar, health numbers, type and distance on the Action Bar.

Install

  1. Download the file from this page and open it with Minecraft.
  2. Create or edit a world and turn on the new pack(s) in its settings.
  3. Enable any Experimental toggles listed under Compatibility, then play.
